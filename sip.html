Student Innovation Project (SIP)

Remote Irrigation Well Control System

Project Overview

A LoRa-based remote shutdown system enabling farmers to safely control irrigation wells from home, even during hazardous weather conditions.

Project Description

Problem Statement: Irrigation wells do not have effective remote shutdown switches. Most lack any remote control because existing commercial solutions are unreliable, extremely expensive ($2,000-5,000+), and require infrastructure that small farms cannot afford.

Why This Matters

  • Safety Risk: Operators must travel to remote well sites in storms or harsh conditions
  • Cost Barrier: Commercial solutions cost $2,000-5,000+ per well
  • Reliability Issues: Existing systems frequently fail when needed most
  • No Scalability: Require expensive infrastructure and subscriptions

Solution Approach

A simple, affordable, and reliable LoRa-based system that connects an Arduino microcontroller at the irrigation well site with a remote control station. Operators can safely shut down wells from home without traveling to dangerous locations.

Innovation Claim

🔧 Affordable LoRa-Based Remote Irrigation Well Control

Unique Innovation: A low-cost ($300), off-grid LoRa radio communication system paired with Arduino microcontrollers that safely controls irrigation wells remotely without internet, cellular, or expensive cloud infrastructure.

Why It's Innovative

  • 10x Cheaper: $300 vs $2,000-5,000 commercial systems
  • Completely Off-Grid: No internet, cellular, or cloud dependency
  • More Reliable: Sub-GHz LoRa technology penetrates obstacles better than alternatives
  • No Subscriptions: One-time cost, no monthly fees
  • Highly Scalable: Add multiple wells without infrastructure changes

Key Advantages Over Existing Solutions

  • 10-15 Mile Range: Covers most agricultural operations
  • 915 MHz Sub-GHz: Penetrates trees, buildings, and weather
  • Battery-Powered: 6+ months per charge per unit
  • Simple Design: Minimal moving parts = fewer failure points
  • Works with Any Well: Gas or electric, new or existing
  • Encrypted Communication: Secure remote operation

Technical Specifications

Hardware

HopeRF RFM95W or Adafruit LoRa Feather, Arduino Nano/Uno, 5V SPDT Relay

Range

10–15 miles Line-of-Sight with high-gain antennas

Frequency

915 MHz (US) - Sub-GHz penetration through obstacles

Power Consumption

Ultra-low: ~2mA standby, 100mA active transmission

Battery Life

6+ months per charge with 12V li-ion battery

Total Cost

Under $300 - 10x cheaper than commercial systems

Communication Protocol

Encrypted LoRaWAN with error checking

Response Time

2-5 seconds from command to engine shutdown
